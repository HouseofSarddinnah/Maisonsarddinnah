Banff and Jasper Itinerary 2026: 7 Days on the Icefields Parkway

The Rockies are at their most memorable when you stop treating the road as a transfer and let the landscape set the pace.

A Banff-to-Jasper road trip can look deceptively simple on a map. The distance is manageable; the experience is not something to hurry. Between the two mountain towns are turquoise lakes, hanging valleys, glaciers, pull-outs that deserve a proper pause and weather that can turn a blue-sky morning into a different day altogether.

This seven-day plan is designed for a first visit in summer or early autumn. It keeps the most famous lakes, gives Banff and Jasper enough time to feel like places rather than hotel addresses, and makes one important choice: the Icefields Parkway gets a full day.

The Route at a Glance

Fly into Calgary and return from Edmonton if flight prices and car hire allow. The most natural order is Calgary airport to Banff, Lake Louise, the Icefields Parkway, Jasper, then Edmonton. A return to Calgary is possible, but it adds a long backtrack at the end.

The Seven-Day Itinerary

Day 1

Arrive in Calgary, settle into Banff

Pick up your car at Calgary International Airport and allow around 90 minutes for the drive to Banff, depending on traffic and weather. Do not over-plan this first day. Check in, walk beside the Bow River, browse Banff Avenue and make your first dinner reservation.

If you arrive early, the Cave and Basin National Historic Site is a thoughtful introduction to the history of Canada's national parks. For a restorative evening, book the Banff Upper Hot Springs rather than adding another scenic drive.

Day 2

Banff without the rush

Start with a gentle walk along the lakeshore at Vermilion Lakes or a short section of the Bow River trail. Then choose one major experience: the Banff Gondola, a guided hike, canoeing where conditions allow, or a spa afternoon.

Keep the evening local. Banff is most enjoyable when you leave space for a proper meal and do not turn every hour into a viewpoint.

Day 3

Lake Louise and Moraine Lake

This is the day that needs the most logistics. Parks Canada says reserved transit is the easy, stress-free and guaranteed way to visit Lake Louise and Moraine Lake, and reservations are required. Check the current shuttle schedule before booking accommodation around it.

Spend the morning at Lake Louise, then take the permitted shuttle onward to Moraine Lake if services are operating. Choose a trail that matches the season and your group; the lakeshore walk is enough to make the day beautiful. Return to Banff or sleep near Lake Louise.

Do not improvise this day

Private vehicle access and parking arrangements change. Use the official Parks Canada Lake Louise and Moraine Lake page for current reservations, closures and shuttle information.

Day 4

The Icefields Parkway: Banff to Jasper

Leave early with a full tank, water and food in the car. The Parkway is not just a scenic link. Plan stops at Bow Lake, Peyto Lake, the Columbia Icefield area and Athabasca Falls, but keep the itinerary light enough that you can stop when the light changes.

The drive is long even before walks, roadworks or wildlife pauses. Check the official road report and weather forecast, and never treat a GPS arrival time as a promise. Arrive in Jasper, check in and have a simple dinner.

Day 5

Jasper's lakes and dark skies

Begin with Medicine Lake and Maligne Lake if roads and conditions are open. A boat cruise is a lovely option, but a lakeshore walk can be just as rewarding and gives you more freedom. Return to Jasper for lunch, then take an easy afternoon at Patricia or Pyramid Lake.

Jasper is one of the world's largest accessible dark sky preserves. On a clear night, turn off the lights, give your eyes time to adjust and let the sky be the evening's main event.

Day 6

A slower Jasper day

Use this as your flexible day. Choose a hike after checking current trail conditions, visit Miette Hot Springs if the road and season suit, or book a guided experience that brings local knowledge into the landscape. Leave time to learn how Jasper is recovering after the 2024 wildfire; the story of a place includes what has been changed as well as what remains.

Day 7

One last morning, then onward

Have breakfast in town, take one final walk, and allow generous time for the drive to Edmonton airport. If your flight is late, the route through the foothills can be a gentler final chapter than trying to add another major attraction. If you are returning to Calgary, add an extra night rather than attempting the entire distance on departure day.

What to Book First

  1. Accommodation. Banff, Lake Louise and Jasper fill early for summer weekends. Compare cancellation policies, not only the headline price.
  2. Lake Louise and Moraine Lake transit. This is the trip's key reservation. Check the official Parks Canada system for the season you are travelling.
  3. Car hire. Choose a vehicle with enough space for layers and food, and confirm winter tires or all-wheel drive if travelling outside summer.
  4. Popular excursions. Maligne Lake cruises, gondolas and guided hikes can sell out. Keep one flexible day so the entire trip does not depend on a single booking.

How to Drive the Icefields Parkway

Fill the tank in Banff or Lake Louise and carry food, water, layers and a paper backup for your route. Phone service is not continuous. Stop only in designated areas, respect wildlife closures and be prepared for cyclists, construction and sudden weather.

The Parkway crosses a fragile landscape. Stay on established paths, never feed animals and keep your distance. A bear, elk or bighorn is not a photo opportunity; it is a wild animal using its habitat.

How Much Does This Trip Cost?

For two people in summer, a comfortable seven-day Rockies trip can easily become a substantial holiday once flights, car hire, accommodation, fuel, meals, park fees and excursions are included. Accommodation is the biggest variable: compare Banff, Canmore, Lake Louise and Jasper, and consider paying more for one memorable night while choosing better-value rooms for the rest.

To manage the budget, book a kitchenette for part of the trip, picnic one Parkway lunch, travel in September if conditions suit, and choose one paid signature experience rather than collecting every ticketed attraction.

Safety, Fire Conditions and Responsible Travel

Check Parks Canada bulletins, current wildfire information, trail conditions, road updates and weather each morning. A route can be open while a particular trail, viewpoint or service is closed. Follow all fire bans and do not use recreational drones in Banff or Jasper national parks; Parks Canada warns that violations can lead to significant fines.

For wildlife, travel in groups where possible, make noise on blind corners, carry bear spray as advised locally and store food properly. Never approach, surround or feed an animal. In the mountains, tell someone your route, take enough water and turn around before conditions become uncomfortable.

The Maison standard

The most luxurious choice in the Rockies is often a little more time: one less attraction, one more night, a proper lunch and enough margin to change plans when the landscape asks you to.
