How to Spend a Sunday in Mexico City

Antiques in the morning, bicycles on Reforma, and a late lunch with nowhere else to be.

Sunday is one of the best days to let Mexico City set the pace. The traffic softens, major avenues become public space and the city feels less like a capital you must navigate and more like a neighborhood you have been invited into.

9:00 — Lagunilla

Start at the Lagunilla antique market with cash, comfortable shoes and a clear sense of your luggage limits. Browse vintage clothing, records and objects with a past. Go early for the calmest experience and negotiate warmly.

12:30 — A Long Lunch

After the market, choose lunch over another attraction. This is the day for a table that takes its time: a dish you have not tried, a cold drink and conversation beyond the original plan.

15:00 — Reforma by Bicycle

When Paseo de la Reforma closes to cars, join the city's cyclists for a different view of its monuments and scale. Check the current route and rental arrangements before you go, wear sunscreen and return the bicycle before the afternoon becomes crowded.

18:00 — One Last Walk

End in Roma or Condesa with a final coffee, a small gallery or a table outside. The best Sunday itinerary is not the one with the most stops. It is the one that leaves you alert enough to remember the color of the evening.
