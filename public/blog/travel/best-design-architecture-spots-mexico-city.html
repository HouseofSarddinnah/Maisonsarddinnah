The Best Design and Architecture Spots in CDMX

A considered itinerary through the homes, hotels, museums and streets that make Mexico City a design capital.

Mexico City is best understood through its materials: volcanic stone, painted plaster, warm wood, polished concrete and the green of an inner courtyard. Design here is not a sealed-off museum subject. It is the texture of an ordinary morning, from a modernist home to the café where you stop afterward.

Casa Luis Barragán

Book ahead and treat the visit as an intimate encounter rather than a quick attraction. Barragán's genius is not only the colour palette; it is the control of movement, light and silence. Allow time for the garden and resist photographing every surface before you have experienced the rooms.

Museums with a Point of View

Pair the Museo Anahuacalli, with its volcanic architecture and pre-Hispanic collection, with the Museo Frida Kahlo in Coyoacán. In Chapultepec, Museo Tamayo and Museo Nacional de Antropología offer very different relationships between art, architecture and landscape.

Design-Forward Hotels

Stay around Roma Norte, Condesa or Polanco depending on your mood. Choose a small hotel with a strong sense of place rather than a generic luxury tower. Look for a central courtyard, thoughtful materials and a neighborhood you will enjoy walking at breakfast and after dinner.

A Practical Route

Spend one morning in Coyoacán, one in Chapultepec and one walking Roma and Condesa. Leave space between appointments. Mexico City's best design moments are often found in a doorway, a small bookshop or a lunch room that was never intended to be an attraction.

The sophisticated way to see CDMX is to notice how the city lives with its architecture. Admire the landmark, then sit down somewhere nearby and let the neighborhood become the main event.
